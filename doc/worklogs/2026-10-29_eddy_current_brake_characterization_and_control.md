## 1. Physical Plant & Objective

The user mounted a solid electrolytic copper flywheel disc to the 5010-360KV outrunner motor can and positioned an adjacent neodymium permanent magnet to serve as a non-contacting eddy current brake. The objective is to eliminate spinup overcurrent faults and achieve stable, continuous closed-loop operation across a multi-speed ladder (300 RPM, 450 RPM, 600 RPM) while annotating all source code with comprehensive mathematical and physical explanations.

### 1.1 Electromechanical Setup
1. **Flywheel Rotor**:
   - Solid copper disc: Mass $m \approx 90\text{ g}$, Radius $R \approx 40\text{ mm}$, Thickness $t \approx 2.0\text{ mm}$.
   - Rotational Inertia:
     $$J = \frac{1}{2} m R^2 \approx \frac{1}{2} (0.090\text{ kg})(0.040\text{ m})^2 = 7.20 \times 10^{-5}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$$
   - This represents an inertia over **$20\times$ higher** than the bare outrunner motor can ($J_{\text{bare}} \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-6}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$).
2. **Eddy Current Brake**:
   - Permanent neodymium magnet generates a transverse magnetic field $\vec{B}$ perpendicular to the rotating copper disc face.
   - By Faraday's and Lenz's laws, rotating the conductor at speed $\omega$ induces circulating eddy currents:
     $$\vec{J}_{\text{eddy}} = \sigma (\vec{\omega} \times \vec{r}) \times \vec{B}$$
   - The resulting Lorentz force $\vec{f} = \vec{J} \times \vec{B}$ creates a braking drag torque proportional to velocity:
     $$\tau_{\text{eddy}}(\omega) = c \cdot B^2 \cdot \omega$$
     behaving as a linear viscous damper across the entire operating speed range.
3. **Power Stage & Circuit Loop**:
   - STM32G474RE Nucleo + X-NUCLEO-IHM07M1 (ST L6230 DMOS 3-phase bridge, 1.4A continuous, 2.2A hardware trip comparator).
   - Total circuit resistance:
     $$R_{\text{loop}} = R_{\text{LL}}(1.00\,\Omega) + 2 R_{DS(\text{on})}(1.46\,\Omega) + 2 R_{\text{shunt}}(0.66\,\Omega) = 3.12\,\Omega$$

## 2. Diagnostic Investigation & Theoretical Breakthroughs

### 2.1 Breakthrough 1: Integer Harmonic Multi-Revolution Reconstruction
- **Observation**: In early tests, occasional optical reflection dropouts caused measured interval $\Delta t$ to double ($2 \times T_{\text{exp}}$). The firmware calculated $60{,}000 / \Delta t$, dropping reported speed by $50\%$ in $1\text{ ms}$ and slamming the governor.
- **Solution**: Implemented integer harmonic pulse reconstruction in `src/motor_control.c`:
  $$k = \text{round}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{T_{\text{exp}}}\right)$$
  For $k \in [2, 5]$, the interval is divided by $k$ ($\Delta t_{\text{eff}} = \Delta t / k$) and $(k - 1)$ revolutions are credited to the odometer. In run `run_20261029_122046`, the motor completed **250 revolutions with ZERO slip** and zero faults.

### 2.2 Breakthrough 2: Root Cause of Startup Inrush Overcurrent
- **Observation**: Adding acceleration feedforward voltage ($V_{\text{accel}} = \Delta n_{\text{error}} \times 0.0040\text{V}$) at standstill added $+1.20\text{V}$ on top of $V_{\text{boost}}$ ($4.24\text{V}$), producing $5.44\text{V}$ across $3.12\,\Omega$ ($1.74\text{A}$ inrush) and tripping the L6230 comparator.
- **Solution**: Gated $V_{\text{accel}}$ to activate only when $n_{\text{rotor}} > 60\text{ RPM}$, once the static breakaway boost ($V_{\text{boost}}$) begins tapering. This completely eliminated startup inrush.

### 2.3 Breakthrough 3: Mathematical Derivation of the 304 RPM Plateau & Torque Angle Slip
- **Observation**: In runs 121649, 122046, 123312, and 124132, the motor smoothly accelerated from 8 RPM to 304 RPM, but plateaued at 304 RPM for 20-40 seconds before stalling, with $I_q$ oscillating between $+78\text{ mA}$ and $-72\text{ mA}$.
- **Root Cause Derivation**:
  In an AC permanent magnet synchronous motor (PMSM), the stator electrical angle $\theta_e(t)$ is generated by integrating electrical frequency:
  $$\theta_e(t) = \int \omega_e(t) dt$$
  If an artificial frequency lead $\Delta \omega > 0$ is continuously added to the stator command:
  $$\frac{d\delta}{dt} = \omega_e(t) - \omega_r(t) = \Delta \omega$$
  The torque angle $\delta(t)$ does NOT stay at a fixed value. It continuously spins out around $360^\circ$ at slip frequency $\Delta f_e = \frac{\Delta n \cdot p}{60}$!
  For $\Delta n = 3.0\text{ RPM}$ at 7 pole pairs:
  $$\Delta f_e = \frac{3.0 \times 7}{60} = 0.35\text{ Hz} \implies T_{\text{slip}} = 2.85\text{ seconds}$$
  Every 2.85 seconds, $\delta$ rotates through:
  - $[0^\circ, 180^\circ]$: Motoring torque ($I_q > 0$).
  - $[180^\circ, 360^\circ]$: Regenerative braking torque ($I_q < 0$).
  Net average torque over each slip period is **ZERO**. The rotor sits in an oscillatory trap at 304 RPM, until eddy brake drag eventually stalls it.
- **Physical Solution**:
  1. Set $\text{target\_lead} = 0.2\text{ RPM}$ (strict synchronism, zero frequency slip). The governor acts as a ceiling clamp to prevent the stator from running away ahead of the rotor.
  2. Increase $V_{\text{eddy}}$ feedforward to $2.20\text{ mA/RPM}$ ($V_{\text{eddy}} = (n \times 2.20 / 1000) \times 3.12\,\Omega$). At 450 RPM, this injects $3.09\text{V}$ ($990\text{ mA}$), supplying the genuine DC motoring current required to accelerate through and beyond 300 RPM.
  3. Increase dynamic acceleration boost $V_{\text{accel}}$ to $0.0035\text{ V/RPM}$ ($1.20\text{V}$ max, providing up to $385\text{ mA}$ extra motoring current during ramp).
