# Optical Tachometer RPM Measurement & Stall Detection Strategy

## 1. Overview & Objective
An optical photodetector has been mounted on the motor test fixture to detect mechanical revolutions via a falling-edge pulse (default HIGH, pulses LOW when marker/aperture passes). 

This document defines:
1. **Physical Pinout & Ground Allocation**: Direct connection points on the X-NUCLEO-IHM07M1 expansion board with zero conflict against existing motor drive signals.
2. **Timer Peripheral Selection**: Architectural evaluation of available STM32G474RE timers, identifying the optimal 32-bit hardware capture timer.
3. **Measurement Strategy & Math**: Precision period-measurement formulas, timer scaling, input filtering, and resolution.
4. **Stall & Vibration Detection**: Analytical detection logic to differentiate active rotation from magnetic lockup, stall, or oscillation/vibration.

## 2. Hardware Interface & Pinout Selection

### 2.1 Connector Pinout: J3 Header (Hall / Encoder Connector)
The X-NUCLEO-IHM07M1 expansion board features a dedicated 5-pin 2.54 mm (0.1") male header designated **`J3`** located along the top edge of the board near the motor screw terminals (J2).

| J3 Pin | Silk Screen / Signal | Connected STM32 Pin | Timer Function | Electrical Protection on IHM07M1 | Recommended Connection |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|:---|
| **Pin 1** | **A+ / H1** | **PA15** | **TIM2_CH1** | 1.8 kΩ series (R30), dual Schottky clamp to 3V3/GND (D2), 10 pF cap (C20) | **Alternative Signal Pin** |
| **Pin 2** | **B+ / H2** | **PB3** | **TIM2_CH2** | 1.8 kΩ series (R31), dual Schottky clamp to 3V3/GND (D3), 10 pF cap (C21) | Available |
| **Pin 3** | **Z+ / H3** | **PB10** | **TIM2_CH3** | 1.8 kΩ series (R32), dual Schottky clamp to 3V3/GND (D4), 10 pF cap (C22) | **PRIMARY RECOMMENDED SIGNAL PIN** (Traditional Index `Z`) |
| **Pin 4** | **+5V** | 5V Power Rail | N/A | Sensor power supply rail (from Nucleo 5V) | Photodetector VCC (if 5V required) |
| **Pin 5** | **GND** | System Ground | N/A | Direct solid plane connection to Nucleo digital & power GND | **PRIMARY RECOMMENDED GROUND PIN** |

> [!TIP]
> **Why J3 is the Ideal Physical Interface:**
> 1. **Co-located Ground & Signal**: J3 Pin 5 (GND) is adjacent to Pin 3 (Signal) and Pin 4 (+5V), allowing standard 2-wire or 3-wire DuPont jumper cables to plug directly into a single header.
> 2. **Built-in Protection**: J3 inputs pass through 1.8 kΩ current-limiting resistors and dual BAT30K Schottky diodes clamped to the 3.3V supply rail and ground, preventing high-voltage spikes from damaging the STM32 MCU.
> 3. **Hardware Filtering**: Onboard 10 pF ceramic capacitors filter out high-frequency EMI generated by the 20 kHz inverter switches.
> 4. **Optional Pull-Up**: If the photodetector has an open-collector / open-drain output, jumper **`JP3`** on the IHM07M1 can be closed to engage onboard 10 kΩ pull-ups, or the STM32's internal pull-up can be enabled in software.

### 2.2 Alternative Connection Points
If the user prefers connecting to Arduino or Morpho headers:
- **Signal Alternative 1 (Arduino D6)**: Arduino header CN9 Pin 7 is wired to STM32 pin `PB10` (same electrical net as J3 Pin 3).
- **Signal Alternative 2 (Morpho CN10 Pin 25)**: Morpho pin CN10 Pin 25 is wired to `PB10`.
- **Signal Alternative 3 (Morpho CN7 Pin 17)**: Morpho pin CN7 Pin 17 is wired to `PA15` (TIM2_CH1).
- **Ground Alternatives**:
  - Arduino CN6 Pin 6 or Pin 7 (`GND`)
  - Arduino CN5 Pin 7 (`GND`)
  - Morpho CN7 Pin 8, 19, 20, 22 (`GND`)
  - Morpho CN10 Pin 9, 20 (`GND`)
  - Power connector J1 Screw Terminal 2 (`GND`)

## 3. Peripheral & Timer Conflict Audit

The STM32G474RE running the X-NUCLEO-IHM07M1 currently utilizes the following peripherals:

| Peripheral | Allocation | Used Pins / Channels | Conflict Status with Tachometer |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **TIM1** | 20 kHz Center-Aligned 3-Phase Inverter PWM & ADC Trigger | PA8 (CH1), PA9 (CH2), PA10 (CH3), PA6 (BKIN Fault) | **DO NOT USE** (Motor drive timing) |
| **TIM16** | Hardware Current Trip Threshold DAC Reference | PB4 (CH1 PWM) | **DO NOT USE** (Overcurrent reference) |
| **ADC1** | Phase Currents, VBUS, Temperature, Potentiometer | PA0, PA1, PC0, PC1, PC2, PB1 | No conflict |
| **LPUART1** | Interactive Serial Console (115200 baud) | PA2 (TX), PA3 (RX) | No conflict |
| **TIM5** | 32-bit General Purpose Timer | Pins PA0, PA1, PA2, PA3 | Conflicted with ADC & Console |
| **TIM3 / TIM4** | 16-bit General Purpose Timers | PC6, PC7, PB6, PB8 | Free, but 16-bit limit overflows quickly at low RPM |
| **TIM2** | **32-bit General Purpose Timer** | **PA15 (CH1), PB3 (CH2), PB10 (CH3), PB11 (CH4)** | **ZERO CONFLICT — 100% AVAILABLE** |

### Why TIM2 is the Optimal Peripheral:
1. **True 32-Bit Counter**: On the STM32G474RE, TIM2 is a full 32-bit hardware timer ($2^{32} - 1 = 4,294,967,295$ counts).
2. **Direct Hardware Input Capture**: Capture/Compare registers (CCR1 on PA15, CCR3 on PB10) capture the free-running counter value with zero CPU latency or interrupt jitter.
3. **No 16-Bit Rollover Bottleneck**:
   - A 16-bit timer clocked at 1 MHz rolls over every 65.5 ms (equivalent to ~915 RPM), requiring complex overflow interrupt chaining to measure low speeds.
   - At a 1 MHz clock, **TIM2 rolls over once every 4,294.9 seconds (~71.5 minutes)**!
   - Minimum measurable speed without rollover handling is $< 0.02\text{ RPM}$.
4. **Modulo 32-Bit Arithmetic**: Unsigned subtraction `(uint32_t)(current_capture - previous_capture)` automatically resolves timer rollover without conditional branching.

## 4. Measurement Strategy & Mathematical Model

### 4.1 Clocking & Timebase Configuration
- **APB1 Timer Clock Frequency**: $f_{\text{APB1\_TIM}} = 170\text{ MHz}$ (since APB1 prescaler = DIV1).
- **Timer Prescaler ($PSC$)**: Configure $PSC = 169$.
  $$f_{\text{timer}} = \frac{170\text{ MHz}}{169 + 1} = 1.000000\text{ MHz} \quad (1\text{ tick} = 1.0\ \mu\text{s})$$
- **Auto-Reload Register ($ARR$)**: $0\text{xFFFFFFFF}$ (free-running 32-bit counter).

### 4.2 Input Capture Configuration
- **Channel**: TIM2 Channel 3 (PB10) or Channel 1 (PA15).
- **Active Edge**: `TIM_ICPOLARITY_FALLING` (Falling edge triggered).
- **Input Selection**: `TIM_ICSELECTION_DIRECTTI`.
- **Input Filter ($ICFilter$)**: Set filter to $4$ or $8$ clock cycles (`0x04` or `0x08`).
  - This hardware digital debouncer filters out spikes shorter than $50\text{ ns}$ caused by 20 kHz BLDC MOSFET switching transients.

### 4.3 RPM Calculation Formula
Let:
- $\Delta\text{ticks} = CCR_{\text{now}} - CCR_{\text{prev}}$ (elapsed microseconds).
- $P$ = Photodetector pulses per revolution ($P = 1$ for a single flag/slot).

The period of one mechanical revolution is:
$$T_{\text{rev}} = \frac{\Delta\text{ticks}}{f_{\text{timer}}} = \frac{\Delta\text{ticks}}{1,000,000}\ \text{seconds}$$

The rotational speed in Revolutions Per Minute (RPM) is:
$$\text{RPM} = \frac{60.0}{T_{\text{rev}} \times P} = \frac{60,000,000.0}{\Delta\text{ticks} \times P}$$

### 4.4 Measurement Resolution & Dynamic Range
| Rotational Speed | Period $T_{\text{rev}}$ | Elapsed Counts ($\Delta\text{ticks}$) | Single-Count Resolution ($\Delta\text{RPM}$) | Relative Accuracy |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **300 RPM** | $200.0\text{ ms}$ | $200,000$ | $0.0015\text{ RPM}$ | $0.0005\%$ |
| **1,000 RPM** | $60.0\text{ ms}$ | $60,000$ | $0.0167\text{ RPM}$ | $0.0017\%$ |
| **3,000 RPM** | $20.0\text{ ms}$ | $20,000$ | $0.1500\text{ RPM}$ | $0.0050\%$ |
| **6,000 RPM** | $10.0\text{ ms}$ | $10,000$ | $0.6000\text{ RPM}$ | $0.0100\%$ |
| **12,000 RPM** | $5.0\text{ ms}$ | $5,000$ | $2.4000\text{ RPM}$ | $0.0200\%$ |

## 5. Stall & Vibration Detection Architecture

When an open-loop or ramping motor stalls or falls out of sync, it often remains locked in a static magnetic detent or vibrates rapidly about an equilibrium angle without performing full mechanical rotations.

### 5.1 Timeout & Zero-Speed Determination
Since input capture interrupts only execute when an optical edge occurs, stopping the motor halts interrupts. If unhandled, the RPM variable would freeze at its last computed value.
- In the deterministic 1 ms control task (`Motor_Update1ms()`):
  $$\Delta t_{\text{quiet}} = \text{now\_ms} - t_{\text{last\_pulse\_ms}}$$
- If $\Delta t_{\text{quiet}} > T_{\text{stall\_timeout}}$ (e.g., $500\text{ ms}$, representing $< 120\text{ RPM}$):
  $$\text{RPM}_{\text{measured}} = 0.0\text{ RPM}$$

### 5.2 Fault State Machine: Distinguishing Running vs. Stalled/Vibrating
The motor controller continuously tracks:
1. $\text{RPM}_{\text{cmd}}$: The commanded electrical speed ($\frac{f_{\text{elec}} \times 60}{\text{pole\_pairs}}$).
2. $\text{RPM}_{\text{optical}}$: The measured physical shaft speed from the photodetector.
3. $I_{\text{rms}}$: Instantaneous phase current.

#### Analytical Diagnostics:
- **Case 1: Normal Rotating Operation**
  - $\text{RPM}_{\text{optical}} \approx \text{RPM}_{\text{cmd}}$ (within $\pm 10\%$).
- **Case 2: Mechanical Stall / Magnetic Lockup**
  - Commanded speed $> 200\text{ RPM}$, but $\Delta t_{\text{quiet}} > 500\text{ ms}$ ($\text{RPM}_{\text{optical}} = 0.0$).
  - Current approaches or hits the continuous limit ceiling (`torque_limit_amps`).
  - Action: Immediately declare `MOTOR_STATE_FAULT` (Stall), disable half-bridges, and illuminate the red fault LED.
- **Case 3: Inverter Slipping / Out-of-Sync Vibration**
  - Commanded speed is ramping up, but $\text{RPM}_{\text{optical}}$ plateaus or drops to zero while acoustic hum/vibration increases.
  - Action: Capture telemetry slip ratio $\eta = \frac{\text{RPM}_{\text{optical}}}{\text{RPM}_{\text{cmd}}}$. If $\eta < 0.2$ for $> 400\text{ ms}$, abort ramp and fold back duty.
